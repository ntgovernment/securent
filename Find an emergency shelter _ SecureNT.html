Find an emergency shelter

Below are maps you can use to find emergency shelters and car park shelters.

You should not go to a shelter until you are told by authorities that they are open.

Go to the alerts and warnings page for the latest updates about emergencies and disasters.

Find an emergency shelter


Public emergency shelters are available for residents:

  • whose homes are at risk from storm surge
  • living in caravan parks or non-coded homes
  • who feel unsafe in their home.

What to take with you

You should take your emergency kit of supplies, a blanket and pillows.

Space ratios in shelters are based on 1.2 square metres per person.

Sheltering with your pets

You can use the car park shelters if you want to shelter in your car with your pets.

Car park shelters are the only shelters you can take pets to.

You should also take your emergency kit.

Car park shelters have very limited access to toilets or any other services.

Pets must remain secured and under your control at all times.

Read about pets in emergencies for more information about how you can prepare your pets.

Emergency bushfire shelters

An emergency bushfire shelter or safe place is a building or large open green area where you can shelter from a bushfire.

Include your emergency shelter or safe place in your bushfire plan. Make sure everyone in your household knows:

  • where it is
  • how far away it is
  • how long it will take to get there.

If you do not know where your nearest emergency shelter is, identify other safe places in your bushfire plan. This could be a shopping centre or oval well away from bushland.

Everyone is responsible for their own safety during a bushfire.

Never drive through fire or smoke to reach an emergency shelter or safe place.

In an emergency, call triple zero (000).
